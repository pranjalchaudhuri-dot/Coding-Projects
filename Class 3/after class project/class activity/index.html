Exploring the Web Educationally

Some useful links for school and research

Jump to the bottom

Navigating the internet for learning can feel overwhelming given the sheer volume of information available.
Having a curated list of verified, high-quality educational portals makes research significantly easier and more reliable.
Below are some of the most trusted digital tools and institutions online, complete with reference citations to validate their authority.



Exploring Space:

Space exploration expands our understanding of physics, engineering, and our place in the universe.
Whether you are tracking real-time satellite telemetry or exploring celestial bodies through high-resolution imaging,
space agency archives and literary databases offer a vast window into astronomy and sci-fi history.

NASA Webpage
Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) — Official U.S. government agency dedicated to air and space science.

Goodreads Webpage
Source: Goodreads (Amazon Subsidiary) — World's largest database for book recommendations, reader reviews, and literary metadata.



Exploring Earth:

Understanding planet Earth requires looking at both physical geography and natural history.
Modern digital mapping tools allow students to travel the globe virtually,
analyzing topography, biodiversity, and historical climate patterns directly from a browser.

National Geographic Webpage
Source: National Geographic Society — World-renowned non-profit scientific and educational organization focusing on geography, archaeology, and natural science.

Google Earth Webpage
Source: Google LLC — 3D globe platform rendering satellite imagery, aerial photography, and GIS data.



Learning to Code:

Computer science and computational thinking have become essential skills across almost every field of study.
Learning to code develops problem-solving capabilities, logical thinking, and creativity.
Interactive platforms simplify syntax and allow beginner programmers to build real projects step-by-step.

Learn more about coding here:
Code.org
Source: Code.org — Non-profit dedicated to expanding computer science education in schools globally.



Learn Something New:

Self-directed learning relies on comprehensive, open-access knowledge repositories.
From structured video courses covering advanced mathematics to community-curated encyclopedia entries,
these platforms enable lifelong learning on virtually any topic imaginable.

Khan Academy Webpage
Source: Khan Academy — Non-profit educational organization providing free personalized learning tools and K-12 aligned lessons.

Wikipedia Webpage
Source: Wikimedia Foundation — Free, multilingual open-collaborative online encyclopedia maintained by a global community of volunteers.
